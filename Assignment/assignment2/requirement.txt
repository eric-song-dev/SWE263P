For this assignment, you will develop a persona and journey map for UXAirways, a fictional airline.

Usually you would want your personas and journey maps to be based on extensive research with potential users. However, for this assignment, you will use a mix a your own experiences, what you can learn from written sources (new articles, web pages, etc.), competitive analysis (looking at other companies' products) to get started, and then use your own imagination (rooted in common sense and believability) to fill in the gaps.

Design Scenario
UXAirways is a growing all-business-class airline promising a boutique customer experience to frequent business travelers who demand the highest levels of service and satisfaction. UXAirways has decided to develop its own mobile app, and they are trying to decide what it should contain and what opportunities there are to provide their boutique experience in electronic form. The team has asked for two documents to help them understand the design possibilities for the app.

First, the team would like a persona for a typical customer of the airline. Who is their customer, and what is it that they want from their airline and their technology? What do they care about?

Second, the team would like a journey map that represents the customer's path from the moment they consider going on a trip through the point that everything having to do with the trip is completed. This includes not only booking the flight, but also services before, during, and after the flight. The goal here is not to present the user flow through the app, but instead to understand the "touchpoints" - those places in the customer's journey that they might use the app.

Assignment Deliverable
When developing your persona and journey map, you may use templates or examples you find online, but all of the content should be your own and in your own words.

You should submit your persona and journey map as a PDF document.

Tips & Tricks
Many templates include information or sections that aren't relevant for this assignment (like marketing channels). Some also include things like sliders for personality scales or computer expertise that are basically meaningless (like this persona of Leonard GreyLinks to an external site.). Make sure the information you include is both relevant and meaningful.
Think carefully about the "scale" of your journey map. Some journey maps focus on the interactions with/inside of an app. David Holden has created a journey map for an airline appLinks to an external site. that starts when the user gets to the app and ends when they have purchased their ticket. Holden's map is a different scale than what you should produce. His is kind of like what you would see if you zoomed in on one section of your map. Your map should not focus on what happens inside the interaction with an app, but instead on the larger process that starts with deciding to travel and ends when all aspects of the customer's interactions around this flight have finished.
Try to imagine all of the parts of the experience for the businessperson, even if they don't directly involve the airline. For example, a business person may not be making their own travel arrangements, or they may need to get reimbursed after a flight.

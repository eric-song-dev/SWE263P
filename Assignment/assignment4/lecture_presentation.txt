### **1. Gestalt Principles**
[cite_start]Human perception is naturally tuned to find connections and groupings, and visual perception operates according to regular rules[cite: 56, 57, 58]. [cite_start]The lecture outlines several Gestalt principles (meaning "the whole is greater than the sum of its parts") that dictate how the mind organizes visual data[cite: 65, 66]:
* **Proximity**: Things that are closer together are perceived as a group. [cite_start]Conceptually related items should be placed closer to each other[cite: 75, 76, 77].
* [cite_start]**Similarity**: Elements that share visual characteristics (like type or color) are perceived as a group[cite: 88, 89, 96].
* [cite_start]**Closure**: The mind perceives disconnected elements as creating whole shapes, which is especially useful for designing logos and icons[cite: 106, 107, 109, 110].
* [cite_start]**Continuity**: Lines that continue in the same direction or follow good alignment are perceived as a cohesive whole[cite: 136, 137, 143].
* [cite_start]**Symmetry**: Symmetrical elements are perceived as related, suggesting balance and stability[cite: 184, 185, 188].
* [cite_start]**Figure-Ground**: Humans instinctively perceive objects to be in the foreground (more important elements) or the background[cite: 212, 213, 214, 215].

### **2. Visual Hierarchy Indicators**
[cite_start]Visual hierarchy uses visual cues to communicate a hierarchical conceptual structure[cite: 53]. [cite_start]The presentation breaks these indicators down into two main categories[cite: 236]:
* **Position**:
    * [cite_start]*Proximity to Frame*: Items near the center generally carry more importance than those near the edges[cite: 251, 252].
    * [cite_start]*Placement & Proximity*: Related items are placed closer together, using whitespace to separate conceptual groupings[cite: 274, 275].
    * [cite_start]*Eye Behavior*: In Western contexts, objects nearer the top and to the left are perceived as more important[cite: 319, 320].
    * [cite_start]*Nesting*: Related objects or things at the same hierarchical level are grouped within the same container[cite: 325, 326, 363, 364].
    * [cite_start]*Overlap*: Objects placed on top or in front of others are viewed as more important or urgent[cite: 392, 393, 394].
* [cite_start]**Treatment**: Hierarchy can also be established using **Size** (different font sizes), **Color** (higher-saturation colors stand out), **Ornament** (adding visual flourishes), and **Finish** (adding visual interest)[cite: 428, 430, 436, 437, 457, 458, 466, 467].

### **3. Prototyping and Wireframing Fidelity**
[cite_start]The lecture covers the spectrum of prototyping, ranging from low to high fidelity[cite: 561].
* [cite_start]**Low-Fidelity (Low-Fi)**: Consists of non-operational, paper-based sketches[cite: 570, 571, 572].
* [cite_start]**Medium to High-Fidelity (High-Fi)**: Ranges from patched-up digital wireframes to highly functional prototypes[cite: 573, 579, 611]. 
    * [cite_start]*Pros of High-Fi*: Allows for testing complex user interactions, spotting strengths/weaknesses, and clearer communication with engineers and investors[cite: 630, 633, 636, 638].
    * [cite_start]*Cons of High-Fi*: It is time-intensive, costly, and can distract designers by making them focus on colors and shapes rather than basic functionality and layout[cite: 639, 640, 645].
